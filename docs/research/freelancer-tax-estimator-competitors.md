# Competitor review: freelancer and creator tax estimators

- **Reviewed:** November 2, 2026
- **Market:** U.S. individual freelancers, self-employed people, and online creators
- **Method:** First-party product pages, calculator pages, and help/methodology pages only. “Observed” means fields and default/result text exposed by the public page’s readable rendering. I could not operate the controls in a live browser, so submitted-input behavior, hidden advanced fields, signup gates after calculation, and mobile rendering are marked unverified. Privacy and tax-method statements below are the companies’ own published claims, not independent audits.

## What the market already covers

The closest match to the planned payment reserve tool is [SetAsideCheck](https://setasidecheck.com/). Its public form already combines filing status, state, annual 1099 income, expenses, household W-2 wages, an optional current payment amount, and advanced household inputs; its result includes a payment-specific reserve, net profit, effective rate, and amount kept. It also says the calculator runs in the browser and stores nothing. The planned product therefore cannot rely on “invoice set-aside,” household context, or browser-only privacy as a unique feature.

Creator-specific positioning is also occupied. [MyCreatorTax](https://mycreatortax.com/influencer-tax-calculator) has creator-type selections and a creator income/expense flow; [Solo Ops Lab](https://soloopslab.com/tools/youtube-ad-revenue-tax-calculator) models YouTube revenue-share choices and possible Google backup withholding; [GigProfitMath](https://gigprofitmath.com/tiktok-creator-tax-calculator/) has TikTok payout, brand-deal, and other creator-income fields. A generic freelancer tool can still serve creators, but “for freelancers and creators” alone is not a defensible niche.

The clearest product opening is a better *combined decision flow*: start with a fast estimate of what to reserve from a payment, then show annual and monthly take-home with a readable tax/expense breakdown and explicit assumptions. Competitors cover parts of that combination, but the public pages reviewed do not show the planned slider-led input, annual/monthly revenue switch, visual breakdown bar, and copyable plain-language summary together. This is an interface opportunity, not evidence that the underlying tax calculation is novel.

## Closest competitors in more detail

### SetAsideCheck: nearest product match

The form and result labels exposed on the [calculator page](https://setasidecheck.com/) closely match the planned core: annual gross receipts and expenses, filing status/state, household wages, optional current check amount, and optional local tax/other income/health-insurance/retirement details. It displays a reserve amount and percentage for each payment, plus profit and amount kept. Its stated state model uses 2026 state brackets (single-filer brackets doubled for joint filers) and warns that state deductions/credits vary. It also says the calculator runs in the browser and stores nothing.

The page continues into a paid spreadsheet tracker offer. The readable form does not show a monthly/annual toggle, sliders, a visual allocation bar, or a copy-summary control. These are plausible UX differences to pursue, but the live controls were not operated, so absence is limited to what the page exposes.

### TheMoneySheet: take-home calculation and transparent limits

The [freelance take-home page](https://themoneysheet.com/calculators/freelance-take-home-pay/) documents the chain from gross revenue through expenses, net profit, federal income/SE tax, optional state estimate, and annual/monthly take-home. It says it assumes a sole proprietor using the standard deduction and one business, and explicitly characterizes state tax as a flat rate entered by the user rather than state-bracket tax. Its separate [privacy/contact page](https://themoneysheet.com/contact/) says financial inputs are not transmitted or stored.

This is a close match to the planned annual/monthly net result. The calculator page’s readable form does not expose W-2/household income or an invoice-specific reserve. The report should treat those as “not visible in this tool’s public form,” not as proof the site has no related tool.

### Creator tools: creator positioning is already served

[MyCreatorTax](https://mycreatortax.com/influencer-tax-calculator) selects among creators and several freelancer roles, takes gross creator income before fees/expenses, and lists creator-relevant expense categories such as equipment, subscriptions, editors, home office, business-use phone/internet, travel, and platform fees. It publishes 2026 federal and state tax handling, quarterly dates, and a “set aside from every brand payment” promise. Its page claims browser-only calculations. A “Have a W-2 job, or know last year’s tax?” prompt appears, but the extracted page did not show enough to verify the exact W-2/withholding inputs.

[Solo Ops Lab’s YouTube tool](https://soloopslab.com/tools/youtube-ad-revenue-tax-calculator) tackles a more specific creator confusion: whether the user’s YouTube number is their AdSense share or gross ad revenue. It separately asks whether the amount is gross or net, accepts channel expenses and household W-2 wages, and asks whether U.S. tax information was submitted to AdSense. Its methodology explains the possible backup withholding treatment and why it is subtracted from quarterly payments. It excludes state/local tax and some filing statuses/credits, as stated on the page.

[GigProfitMath’s TikTok tool](https://gigprofitmath.com/tiktok-creator-tax-calculator/) separates TikTok payouts, brand deals, and other creator income, includes a simplified home-office input, and offers either state brackets or a custom state rate. It says local taxes are excluded. Its homepage describes a broader library for delivery drivers, creators, sellers, and freelancers, and claims browser calculation, no stored data, and no signup ([homepage](https://gigprofitmath.com/)).

Together, these make “creator income categories” a baseline expectation in some segments. A defensible creator-specific edge would need to solve a particular workflow such as platform payout vs. gross reporting, withholding already taken, and a usable payment-by-payment reserve. The planned general estimator can include creator use cases through examples and flexible income labels without turning the first screen into a platform-specific tax interview.

### HourFloor: strong state model, reverse calculation

[HourFloor](https://hourfloor.com/) works backwards from desired take-home to required billings and hourly/day/week/month rates. Its exposed form includes state, filing status, billable hours, weeks off, monthly expenses, health insurance, retirement, and advanced retirement/QBI-related inputs. Its displayed breakdown separates SE, federal, state tax, business costs, health/retirement, and take-home; it has a “Copy link to this result” control. The [methodology page](https://hourfloor.com/methodology) describes state-specific tax rules, QBI handling, and household tax-difference treatment when a spouse has W-2 wages.

This is a strong benchmark for transparent assumptions and state freshness. Its main job is rate planning rather than “what should I hold from this payment?” The visible form did not expose a payment amount or household W-2 field, so the exact user path for that spouse-income calculation was not verified.

## Larger-brand and workflow benchmarks

- [Keeper](https://www.keepertax.com/1099-tax-calculator/texas) combines W-2 and 1099 income, state, filing status, business deductions, withholding, and estimated payments in the visible form/result labels. This is broader than a pure 1099-profit calculator and demonstrates that a state selector plus a readable tax breakdown is expected. The advanced fields were not expanded in this review.
- [QuickBooks](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/free-self-employment-tax-calculator/) offers a narrow public SE-tax calculation from net income only and says it excludes income and other taxes; its expenses explanation tells users to subtract expenses first. It directs users toward QuickBooks Self-Employed for quarterly estimates based on tracked income and expenses. This leaves room for a free standalone end-to-end planner.
- [Bonsai](https://www.hellobonsai.com/tax-calculators/self-employment-united-states) shows that state + filing status + 1099 + W-2 + expenses is a familiar basic form, but its public page’s 2025 title and December 2024 update date underscore the value of displaying the tax year and verification date prominently.
- [Found](https://found.com/help/taxes/how-is-my-tax-bill-calculated) turns estimation into ongoing account-based bookkeeping. Its estimate uses a tax profile, logged income/expenses and deductions, and a state/municipal flat percentage; its [Taxes pocket](https://found.com/help/taxes/how-does-my-taxes-account-work) can allocate a chosen/suggested percentage from deposits. This is a different tradeoff from an anonymous one-off tool.

## Implications for the product plan

1. **Do not claim the invoice reserve itself as unique.** SetAsideCheck already offers the closest version, and MyCreatorTax advertises a reserve from every brand payment. Compete on the *combined* experience and presentation: annual/monthly income mode, current-payment amount, take-home, visual allocation, copyable summary, and concise explanation of what is estimated.
2. **Make W-2 support deliberate.** SetAsideCheck and HourFloor address household wages; Solo Ops Lab and MyCreatorTax surface W-2 context for creator income; Keeper and Bonsai include W-2 fields. For a general estimator, distinguish the user’s W-2 wages, spouse wages for joint filers, and withholding already paid. If these are postponed to an optional advanced section, say what the simple estimate assumes.
3. **Use actual filing statuses.** Competitor forms label filing status as Single, Married Filing Jointly, etc. The [IRS lists five filing statuses](https://www.irs.gov/filing/filing-status?lv=true); LLC is handled separately under federal entity-classification rules that depend on ownership and elections ([IRS LLC classification guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/llc-filing-as-a-corporation-or-partnership)). “LLC / Self-Employed” should not be a filing-status option. Keep sole-proprietor/self-employment assumptions separate from filing-status selection; add other entity treatment only if the calculation supports it.
4. **Choose a state strategy with explicit fidelity.** Competitors span user-entered flat percentages (TheMoneySheet, FreelancerTaxEstimator, Found), state brackets (SetAsideCheck, HourFloor, MyCreatorTax, GigProfitMath), and a very limited no-state model (Solo Ops Lab, QuickBooks). If using a flat approximation or preset, label it plainly and expose the method/source date; do not make a generic state selector imply complete state return accuracy.
5. **Privacy is a promise to substantiate, not a differentiator by itself.** Several competitors claim in-browser calculation and no data storage. If adopted, the implementation and analytics configuration must support the product’s exact privacy wording. Avoid claiming “no data leaves your browser” if analytics, embedded vendors, or error reporting can receive calculator values.
6. **Freshness and assumptions can build trust.** Several competitors publish tax year, sources, and/or a verification date; Bonsai’s stale page shows how visible date cues can matter. Give every estimate a year, a “last reviewed” marker, named assumptions, and a short disclaimer near the result.
7. **Creator specificity can be an optional example layer.** The surveyed creator tools cover gifts/brand deals, platform fees, creator rewards, and YouTube withholding mechanics. A broad first screen can stay lightweight while its labels/examples explain that sponsorships, affiliate income, subscriptions, and platform payouts are self-employment income. Platform-specific gross/net and withholding logic should be added only if the scope includes maintaining those rules.

## Research questions resolved in the current spec

These questions were open when this competitor review was captured. The Grill with Docs phase subsequently resolved them; see the [current product specification](../specs/2026-11-03-setaside-tax-estimator.md) and ADRs 0001–0020.

- Is the intended primary user a full-time sole proprietor, a W-2 side-hustler, or both? That determines whether household wages and withholding belong in the first result or behind “more details.”
- Is the state output a transparent user-entered percentage, an approximate state-bracket model, or a later phase? The closest competitors already cover both approaches, so the choice should follow tax-maintenance capacity and trust requirements.
- Should the product estimate only current-year tax reserve, or also explain quarterly payments/safe harbor? Competitors commonly bundle quarterly amounts, but doing so increases scope and tax-calendar maintenance.
- Are creators a launch audience or one example segment? Dedicated creator calculators now exist; if they are a core audience, select an exact problem (for example platform withholding or gross-versus-payout reconciliation) to support.
